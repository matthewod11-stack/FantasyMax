# Year 11 Draft Analysis

GENTLEMEN!

Year 11 will be underway in a few short hours.  My thoughts on our drafts below:

Purple Reign

Started with Ja’Marr Chase at 1, hoping he picks up right where he left off last season. George Kittle at 28 might have been a touch early, but doubling down with Burrow at 29 was the strong stack move that could pay off big. Jerry Jeudy and George Pickens in the middle rounds are fine swings, though neither screams league-winner.

ODEEZUS

Bijan at 2 is as safe as it gets. Brock Bowers at 27 made him the first tight end off the board, and while aggressive, it could be the kind of swing that defines a season. Jaxon Smith-Njigba and Calvin Ridley in the 3rd and 4th were clear bets — one breakout candidate, one bounce-back vet. Does Ekeler still have the juice? Charbonnet, Blue, and Ayomanor are upside darts that could make this roster look brilliant or barren by midseason. But as this owner loves to remind us, championships are *won on the wire (*has won no championships).

Feeling Good

Saquon at 3 is a steady anchor. Bucky Irving at 26 is a great pick. Zay Flowers and Tank Bigsby in the middle were fine bets and will play a huge role in whether this squad can rebound from last year’s heartbreaking championship defeat. The weekly Herbert vs. Nix decision looms, and it could swing their season.

FieldIt2Me

Opened with Gibbs and AJ Brown, a dream start. Followed up with Garrett Wilson, who might be dealing with far fewer catchable balls this season. Rhamondre in the 9th was sneaky value and could end up being the key depth piece. The big question: can Baker really push for 40+ TDs?

Malört

Derrick Henry at 5 was a big bet, and also the first 30+ RB off the board. This guy fucking loves Ladd McConkey... Jalen Hurts at 33 was a no brainer. Add Davante Adams in Round 4 and Chris Godwin in the 7th, and this is one old team banking on one more last gasp this season.

theACEboogie1

Christian McCaffrey falling to 6 was a gift — or maybe a curse. With him it feels like a coin flip every season. Tee Higgins in the 3rd was steady, and Kyler Murray in the 8th is the big swing. If he’s back to form, that’s a steal. The rest of the draft leaned young and unproven, which leaves this roster walking a fine line between fresh legs and straight-up inexperience.

MoDitkaMoJohnson

CeeDee Lamb at 7 and Drake London in the 2nd was a strong WR start. Then came Omarion Hampton in the 3rd — the biggest early reach of the draft, at least a round or two ahead of ADP. DJ Moore in the 5th and Caleb Williams in the 7th were bold moves, big bets on Bears that only a homer could make. Kyle Pitts in the 10th still feels like great value, even though deep down we all know he’ll break another owner’s heart.

Game of Jones

Justin Jefferson, Brian Thomas Jr., and Tyreek Hill — that WR trio is pure firepower. Then came Mahomes in the 4th, because why not lean all the way into air raid mode. The problem: no RB until Round 5 with Isiah Pacheco, and it shows. Swift and Javonte are fine but hardly reliable anchors. Rashee Rice in the 8th was a big gamble knowing he’ll miss the first 6 weeks. The path is clear: stack wins behind WR and QB dominance, survive the RB mess, and hope to make the playoffs with 4 dominant WRs + 2 QB stack options..

Joe Buck Yourself

Opened with Amon-Ra, then stacked RBs with Achane and Breece Hall — arguably the most sensible and strategic start of the entire draft. Mike Evans in the 4th kept the floor high. Stefon Diggs in the 6th was the stretch, taken a round early and banking on a bounce-back. And of course, this roster is already calling for a big Drake Maye breakout year.

KnickerBockers

Jonathan Taylor and James Cook at the turn gave them a safe RB-RB foundation, and Marvin Harrison Jr. in the 3rd could be the crown jewel if he bounces back from a down rookie year. The big bet is Brock Purdy — not just efficient, but a true fantasy QB1. David Montgomery in the 7th was a pick to love, adding more stability to an already steady roster.

Laces Out

Ashton Jeanty at 11 was a touch early — or maybe a touch late? You know you’re getting old when you watch him dominate and still think, “he really should be bent with his hands on his knees in the backfield.” Nico Collins in the 2nd and Jayden Daniels in the 3rd were sharp picks, and Hollywood Brown could end up being a massive value. This roster is built well, and if the pieces click, they’ll be tough to beat.

Victorious Secret

Chase Brown at 12 feels like the biggest Round 1 reach. Josh Allen in the 2nd added the best player in football, and Travis Kelce in the 6th was a nostalgia play that may or may not have legs left. Love the Terry McLaurin and TreVeyon Henderson picks in the middle rounds for balance. As the reigning champ, Victorious Secret can afford to gamble — but defending a title on the back of Chase Brown is a bold way to try it.

HOODIECRU

Malik Nabers at 13 was a solid start, and Lamar Jackson in the 2nd gave them one of the best 2 man floors in the league. DK Metcalf and Alvin Kamara add reliable veteran production, while Travis Hunter in the 5th was the fun upside swing. The wheels came off a bit with three tight ends between Rounds 6 and 8 — Mark Andrews, Tyler Warren, and Colston Loveland.  Hollywood couldn’t script a better HoodieCru draft strategy, with the season riding squarely on those massive TE investments.

HR NiGhTmaRe

Puka Nacua and Josh Jacobs at the turn gave this team a strong foundation. Kenneth Walker and Sam LaPorta in the next two rounds added upside at RB2 and a top-tier TE. Chris Olave, Deebo Samuel, and Cooper Kupp round out one of the best WR rooms in the league. Justin Fields in the 9th could be a league-winner. Simply put: this team is tough.
